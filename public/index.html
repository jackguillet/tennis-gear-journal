      How it works

      A tapped string bed rings like a drum skin at one main pitch, usually 450–700 Hz. Tension rises with the square of that pitch, the head area and the weight per metre of the string (its gauge and material). The app uses the drum-skin formula for an oval head, which Cross and Bower checked against a load cell inside a strung racket and found within a few percent.

      That gives the tension left in the strung racket, shown under the big number as "in the bed". It is 30–40% lower than the tension set on the stringing machine, even straight after stringing, because the strings relax and lose tension through the grommets. Apps like RacquetTune show this bed number. The big number turns it back into stringing-machine terms using that typical drop, so it can be compared with what you asked your stringer for. Calibrate right after a restring and the app learns your stringer's real drop. Either way the trend for one racket is reliable: use it to see when to restring.

      Getting a clean reading. Hold the racket by the handle and keep the strings free. Point the phone's bottom edge (the main microphone) at the strings from 10–20 cm, somewhere quiet. Tap the centre of the strings firmly with the heel of your palm, a knuckle or the edge of another racket, and lift off at once: anything left on the strings stops the ring. Tap 3–5 times about a second apart. If the app says a tap was too loud, move the phone a little further away.

      Any recent iPhone or Android phone is accurate enough: their microphones are flat across the 300–1000 Hz band these rings fall in, and their clocks are exact to a few parts per million, so the pitch comes out within about 0.1–0.5%. What limits accuracy is the tap: centre taps, a quiet room and several taps matter more than the phone. The app turns off the phone's voice processing (echo cancelling, noise reduction and automatic volume) so the ring reaches it untouched. You can also upload a recording: in Voice Memos, tap Share, choose Save to Files, then pick it from Upload a recording.

                Strings

                What this is based on

                  The estimate takes the largest of four limits: when your string type goes dead for a player of your level and style, when it is likely to break (gauge, pattern, head size, tension and spin all change how fast strings notch), how much tension your readings show it has lost since stringing, and how old the strings are. Hours are weighted by type of play: drills and ball machine wear strings faster than doubles. Set your level and style in Settings, and save a reading right after each restring, for the best estimate. Each time you press Strings broke, the break estimate moves toward how long strings really last for you.

                        On iPhone: tap Copy, then in Safari bookmark any page (Share, Add Bookmark) and name it "STC courts". Open Bookmarks, tap Edit, open that bookmark and paste over its address. On stc.gametime.net, type "STC courts" in the address bar and tap the bookmark.

                        The button holds a private key that can only add court bookings to your account. Make a new one to turn the old one off.

                        Quick logLog a session from a Shortcut or Home Screen button

                        A private link that can only add (and undo) play sessions.

                        Open the page

                        One-tap Shortcut: in the Shortcuts app, tap +, add Get Contents of URL, paste your link, set Method to POST and Request Body to JSON with kind (Text) = singles and minutes (Number) = 90, then add Show Result. Other kinds: doubles, hitting, drills, ball_machine, serves. Add a racket field (for example v8) to pick a racket.

                        Simpler: open the page in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. It opens to big buttons for type and time.

                        Chat assistantLog play and book courts by chatting with Claude

                        Say "I played an hour of singles". In Claude, open Settings, Connectors, Add custom connector, and paste this as the server URL. Claude then sends you here to sign in and allow it. No secret goes in the link.

                        API tokensFor Claude Code, the Claude API and scripts

                        A token opens the chat connector for apps that send it as a header. Read only tokens can look but not change anything. Anyone with a token can use it, so keep it to yourself.

                        Other apps: send Authorization: Bearer and the token to the connector URL. An app that can't send headers can use /api/mcp/ followed by the token as its URL.
